Digestion, zone by zone

A meal is three cascades run side by side: starch → maltose → glucose, protein → proteoses/peptones → dipeptides → amino acids, fat → di/monoglycerides → fatty acids and glycerol. Every arrow is its own rate equation, and every rate is gated by the same rule: an enzyme only turns over its substrate while the zone it is in has the pH that enzyme needs. Nothing here is timed to "look right" — the mouth's amylase stalling the moment the bolus meets stomach acid, and pepsin doing the same the moment it meets the intestine's alkali, both fall out of one pH function, never a script.
