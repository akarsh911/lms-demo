Organic structures and mechanisms

Two things you usually take on trust. First, a C–C bond turns, but not freely: turn one in 3-D, and the energy of every conformation comes from the crowding of the atoms, by a law you can change. Heat it and count how often it flips; then try it with a double bond. Second, a haloalkane swaps its halogen for OH by one of two paths. Run thousands of molecules at random through both, then measure the rate law and the product’s shape yourself.
