The feasible region

Every constraint of a linear programming problem is a straight line that cuts the plane in two, and the points that obey all of them form the feasible region. Slide the objective line Z = ax + by across it and watch where it leaves: that is the best plan. Check it with the book's corner point method, then move the constraints yourself: make two corners tie, open the region up so it runs off forever, or squeeze it until nothing is feasible at all.
