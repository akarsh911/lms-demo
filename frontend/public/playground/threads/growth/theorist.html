Growth and its regulators

A coleoptile's tip makes auxin all the time and pumps it down the shoot, compartment by compartment, each with its own turnover. Light shifts the flow toward the shaded side; because auxin drives elongation, that side grows faster and the tip leans into the light, straightening out once it faces it. Cut the tip off and the lateral bud beneath it — no longer starved of auxin — grows out on its own. A second tool integrates the chapter's own growth-rate equations into the sigmoid every culture traces.

The chapter's other regulators
  • Gibberellins (GA₃): elongate the internode, delay senescence; spraying sugarcane can add up to 20 t/acre.
  • Cytokinins: made where cells divide; overcome apical dominance and delay leaf senescence.
  • Ethylene: the fruit‑ripening gas; also the "triple response" and breaking of dormancy.
  • Abscisic acid: closes stomata, induces dormancy — the "stress hormone", an antagonist to GA.
