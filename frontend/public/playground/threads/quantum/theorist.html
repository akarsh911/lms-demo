Photons and the atom

Light on a metal knocks electrons out — but only some light. Run the photocell yourself: change the colour, the brightness and the collector’s potential, sweep out the current, measure stopping potentials and fit a straight line to find Planck’s constant, as Millikan did. Then drop hydrogen’s electron between Bohr’s levels and read the light it gives out, and stretch its orbit until the electron’s own wave fits.
