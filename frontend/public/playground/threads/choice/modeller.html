The problem of choice

A farmer's eight fields can grow wheat or barley, but not unlimited amounts of both — land, once given to one crop, cannot also grow the other. Set how good each field is at each crop, then slide how much land goes to wheat: the frontier of what the farm can produce is traced out by actually re-sorting the fields, not drawn in. Read the opportunity cost of moving along it, and see what happens to a plan that wastes land or asks for the impossible.

From the chapter — Building Blocks in Economics
Scarcity
Resources (land, labour, capital) are limited, but wants are not — so choices must be made.
Opportunity cost
The value of the next best alternative given up when a choice is made.
Three questions every economy answers
What to produce, how to produce it, and for whom.
Market, planned and mixed economies
Markets let buyers and sellers decide; planned economies leave it to the government; most economies today, including India since the 1991 reforms, are mixed.
