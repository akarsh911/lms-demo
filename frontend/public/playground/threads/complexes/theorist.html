Colour from split d-orbitals

Surround a metal ion with six ligands and its five d-orbitals split into two groups. How far apart they split, Δo, is computed here from the metal and the ligand together — the same product rule (Jørgensen's) that reproduces the measured splittings for real complexes. Whether the electrons spread out or pair up falls out of comparing two total energies, not a rule of thumb; the colour comes from the light that gap absorbs; and every distinct way six ligands can sit on the six corners of an octahedron, mirror images included, is found by checking which arrangements a rotation can turn into each other.
