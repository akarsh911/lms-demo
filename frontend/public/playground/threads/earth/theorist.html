Inside the Earth, and its moving plates

No one has been below 12 km, yet we know the Earth has a liquid outer core 2,900 km down. Here you find out how. Each earthquake wave is traced step by step through the layers, bending as the speed changes (Snell’s law on a sphere: r·sin i / v stays the same along a ray). Change the laws — make the core solid, move it, or give the mantle one speed — and measure what the seismographs would record. Then move plates apart and together, and read the sea floor’s magnetic stripes and the earthquakes they leave.
