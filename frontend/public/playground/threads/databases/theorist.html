Tables and SQL

Two small tables: Student (RollNo is its primary key) and Club, where RollNo points back to Student — a foreign key. Write a real SQL SELECT, or pick one of the examples, and a small relational engine runs it in stages you can see: a JOIN first builds the full cross product of both tables (every row of one paired with every row of the other), then keeps only the pairs its ON condition matches — a join is an actual cross-match, not a picture of one. GROUP BY buckets rows before an aggregate is worked out on each bucket, and HAVING filters buckets afterwards. Try adding a row that breaks a key, too (CBSE Class 12 Computer Science: relational model, keys, SQL, joins and aggregates).
