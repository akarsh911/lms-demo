Selection and drift

A population of peppered moths on a tree trunk, one generation after another. Birds eat the moths they can see against the bark. The survivors breed at random, and each moth gets two alleles drawn by chance from their gene pool. Nothing tells the allele frequencies where to go. They move because of who was eaten and who happened to be drawn, or they stay still, as Hardy and Weinberg said they would. Blacken the bark, shrink the population, let migrants in, and measure what happens.
