The grapher

Type a law and watch it. Any letter you use that is not x becomes a slider, so a sin(bx + c) arrives with three of them. Everything the page tells you — a root, a turning point, a slope, an area — is found on the curve that is actually drawn, never from a formula about it.

How to drive it

  • Zoom — scroll on the paper. shift+scroll stretches x only, alt+scroll y only.
  • Pan — drag the paper. fit asks the curves where they are; square makes one unit of x the same length as one unit of y, so a circle looks like a circle.
  • trace — drag along a curve and read the pair off. Arrow keys step, shift steps finer.
  • points — marks every root, turning point and crossing in view, found by scanning and bisecting.
  • tangent — drag the ring; the slope is a central difference on the sampled curve.
  • area — drag either edge, then push the strips from 1 to 200 and watch the sum close on the true value.
  • Writing it — 2x, 3sin x and x(x+1) all work without a ×. |x| is the size of x. pi and e are themselves. Angles are in radians.
