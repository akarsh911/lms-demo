World population: the transition, measured

Two models. In the first, a country is counted age by age, every year from 1800 to 2150. You set the law of its demographic transition: when deaths begin to fall, how long births wait before they follow, and where both end up. The birth rate, death rate, doubling time and pyramid are measured from the counts. In the second, people settle an imaginary continent, pushed away from harsh places and pulled towards good ones, and you measure its density and distribution.
